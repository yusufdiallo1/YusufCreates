## 1. `01-why-it-looks-cheap` — Why your UI looks cheap

**Caption**

> Your app works. It still looks cheap. It's almost never the layout.
>
> It's five details nobody thinks to name:
>
> → DEPTH. Stacked drop shadows read as stickers. Real depth comes from a
> surface ladder and a hairline border — each layer a step lighter than the one
> beneath it.
>
> → TYPE. Display text at 90px with default letter-spacing looks loose and
> amateur. Tighten your big sizes, leave body text alone. That one change does
> more than switching typeface.
>
> → COLOUR. Pure #000 makes everything above it look like it's floating in a
> void. A near-black with a hint of blue gives the whole interface somewhere to
> sit.
>
> Here's the part that matters: every one of these is a token you set once, not
> something you fix screen by screen. That's the whole difference between having
> a design and having a design system.
>
> Which one is your app guilty of?
>
> 📌 Save this before your next build.

## 3. `03-how-i-work` — Process

**Caption**

> Most projects die in the deck phase. Here's the four-step version that
> doesn't.
>
> ① FRAME — One sentence, or we don't start. If the problem can't be said in a
> sentence, it isn't understood yet. Scope, stack, and everything we cut all
> fall out of getting that line right.
>
> ② SYSTEM — Tokens before screens. Colour, type, spacing, radius, decided once
> in one file. Screens designed one at a time start drifting by screen three. A
> system doesn't drift.
>
> ③ SHIP — Live URL in week one. Rough is fine, invisible is not. Feedback on
> something you can open on your phone beats feedback on a mockup every single
> time.
>
> ④ SHARPEN — Then make it feel expensive. Motion, contrast, empty states, the
> copy nobody reads until it's wrong. Most people skip this part. It's the part
> people actually notice.
>
> If your project has been "almost ready" for a month, you're stuck in step 2.
>
> 📌 Save this one.
