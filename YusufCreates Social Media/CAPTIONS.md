## 1. `01-why-it-looks-cheap`

> Your app works. It still looks cheap. It's almost never the layout.
>
> It's five details nobody thinks to name:
>
> → DEPTH. Stacked drop shadows read as stickers. Real depth comes from a
> surface ladder and a hairline border — each layer a step lighter than the one
> beneath it.
>
> → TYPE. Display text at 90px with default letter-spacing looks loose and
> amateur. Tighten your big sizes, leave body text alone.
>
> → COLOUR. Pure #000 makes everything above it float in a void. A near-black
> with a hint of blue gives the interface somewhere to sit.
>
> Every one of these is a token you set once, not something you fix screen by
> screen. That's the difference between a design and a design system.
>
> Which one is your app guilty of?

## 3. `03-how-i-work`

> Most projects die in the deck phase. Here's the four-step version that
> doesn't.
>
> ① FRAME — One sentence, or we don't start. If the problem can't be said in a
> sentence, it isn't understood yet.
>
> ② SYSTEM — Tokens before screens. Screens designed one at a time drift by
> screen three. A system doesn't drift.
>
> ③ SHIP — Live URL in week one. Rough is fine, invisible is not.
>
> ④ SHARPEN — Then make it feel expensive. Motion, contrast, empty states, the
> copy nobody reads until it's wrong.
>
> If your project has been "almost ready" for a month, you're stuck in step 2.

## 8. `08-pricing-flat`

> I stopped charging per page. Here's why.
>
> The old model charged $450 for every page above three. So a nine-page site
> came to $4,500 — more than my web app tier. That's absurd, and it was my own
> pricing doing it.
>
> Worse: it made adding a page a negotiation. A client wanting one more page had
> to have a conversation about money instead of a decision about the site.
>
> Now there are two numbers. Three pages is $750. Four through nine is $950 —
> flat, however many you end up needing.
>
> Why it works: most of the cost is design, build setup and deployment, none of
> which scale with page count. The ninth page genuinely isn't much more work
> than the fifth. Charging as if it were was never honest.
>
> Pricing shouldn't make the work worse.

## 10. `10-five-mistakes`

> 5 things killing your landing page. Every one is costing you signups right
> now.
>
> 01 — Your headline describes you. "We're a platform for…" Nobody cares what
> you are. Say what changes for them, in their words, in the first six.
>
> 02 — Five CTAs is the same as none. Every extra button splits attention. One
> primary action per screen.
>
> 03 — A carousel nobody swipes. You built five panels and almost everyone saw
> one. Pick the best and delete the rest.
>
> 04 — It's slow. Every extra second of load costs conversions.
>
> 05 — It's unreadable on a phone. Most of your traffic is outdoors in daylight.
> Check your contrast there, not in a dark room at night.
>
> 📌 Save this before your next launch.

## 11. `11-accessibility`

> Your site fails for roughly 1 in 12 men. Colour blindness affects about 8% of
> them — and it's the easiest problem on this list to fix.
>
> CONTRAST — Grey on grey isn't elegant, it's unreadable outdoors. Body text
> needs 4.5:1. Large text and icons can sit at 3:1, not less.
>
> THE TRAP — One accent colour usually can't do both jobs. Mine passes 3:1 for
> borders and icons but not 4.5:1 under white text, so there's a second darker
> token used only for filled buttons.
>
> KEYBOARD — Tab through your own site right now. If you can't see where you
> are, or you get trapped in a modal, neither can anyone relying on a keyboard
> or a screen reader.
>
> Accessibility is in every tier I sell, not an upsell on the expensive one.
> Building it in from the start costs nothing. Retrofitting it costs a rebuild.
